Important: This table is for educational awareness only. Symptoms can have many causes. Medicines and dosages should not be self-prescribed. Consult a qualified healthcare professional, especially for children, pregnant people, elderly people, or severe symptoms.
Sr. No. Disease / Condition Common Symptoms Possible Cause General Treatment / Medicine Dosage Precautions
1 Common Cold Runny nose, sneezing, sore throat, cough and mild fever. Usually caused by respiratory viruses. Rest, fluids and symptomatic treatment. Paracetamol may be used for fever or pain when appropriate. Use only according to the package directions or advice of a healthcare professional. Wash hands, cover coughs and sneezes, and avoid close contact when sick.
2 Diarrhoea Frequent loose stools, stomach cramps and weakness. Often caused by contaminated food or water, or infection. Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) is important to prevent dehydration. Medical evaluation may be needed depending on the cause. Prepare and drink ORS according to the instructions printed on the packet. Drink safe water, wash hands and maintain food hygiene. Seek medical care for blood in stool, severe dehydration or persistent diarrhoea.
3 Malaria Fever, chills, sweating, headache and weakness. Infection with Plasmodium parasites transmitted by infected mosquitoes. Requires specific antimalarial treatment selected by a healthcare professional after diagnosis. Dose depends on the malaria species, patient's age, weight and prescribed medicine. Prevent mosquito bites using nets, repellents and removal of stagnant water. Seek medical care promptly for suspected malaria.
4 Dengue High fever, headache, body aches, nausea and rash. Dengue virus transmitted by infected Aedes mosquitoes. Supportive care and adequate fluids. Medical assessment is important because severe dengue can develop. Medication should be taken only according to medical advice. Prevent mosquito bites. Avoid aspirin or ibuprofen when dengue is suspected unless a healthcare professional specifically advises otherwise.
5 Typhoid Prolonged fever, weakness, headache, abdominal discomfort and sometimes diarrhoea or constipation. Infection with Salmonella Typhi bacteria, commonly spread through contaminated food or water. Requires medical diagnosis and prescribed antibiotics. Antibiotic choice and dose must be decided by a healthcare professional. Drink safe water, eat properly cooked food and wash hands regularly.
6 Respiratory Infection Cough, sore throat, fever, runny nose or difficulty breathing. Can be caused by viruses or bacteria. Treatment depends on the cause. Antibiotics should not be taken unless prescribed for a bacterial infection. Depends on the diagnosed condition and prescribed medicine. Maintain ventilation, wash hands and avoid sharing personal items with someone who is sick.
7 Skin Infection Itching, redness, swelling, rash or skin lesions. May be caused by bacteria, fungi, parasites or irritation. Treatment depends on the cause. Appropriate antifungal, antibacterial or other treatment may be required. Depends on the diagnosis and medicine prescribed. Keep skin clean and dry and avoid sharing towels, clothes or personal items.
8 Worm Infection Abdominal discomfort, poor appetite, weakness or itching around the anus. Intestinal parasitic worms, often associated with contaminated soil, food or water. Deworming medicine may be used when appropriate, according to local health guidance. Depends on the medicine, age and condition. Follow healthcare or package instructions. Wash hands, keep nails clean, use toilets and wear footwear where appropriate.
9 Conjunctivitis Red eyes, watering, irritation and discharge. Can be caused by viruses, bacteria or allergies. Treatment depends on the cause. Antibiotic eye drops should not be used without appropriate medical advice. Depends on the diagnosed cause and prescribed treatment. Avoid touching the eyes, wash hands frequently and do not share towels.
10 Anaemia Tiredness, weakness, dizziness and shortness of breath during activity. Can result from iron deficiency, blood loss, nutritional deficiency or other medical conditions. Treatment depends on the cause. Iron supplements may be prescribed when iron deficiency is confirmed. Dose should be determined by a healthcare professional. Maintain a balanced diet containing iron-rich foods and seek medical evaluation for persistent symptoms.
